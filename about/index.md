## The Journey

Started as Linux Sysadmin in 2005, spent about 7 years working for [Rompetrol](https://www.rompetrol.com/), eventually led a team of 5 Sysadmins at [EveryMatrix](https://everymatrix.com/). Joined [Pond5](https://www.pond5.com/) in 2017 (later acquired by [Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/)) — all infra was in AWS, so I had to quickly pick up cloud skills and made the mental shift into DevOps philosophy and platform engineering. Been all-in on Kubernetes since 2021.

[KubeCon Amsterdam 2023](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/archive/2023/kubecon-cloudnativecon-europe/) was my first — felt like a kid in a candy store. Skipped most talks, couldn't leave the booth area. Spent the whole time talking to speakers, developers, the people who actually build the stuff I use daily. That was my biggest takeaway. Also met my #1 influencers [Viktor Farcic](https://www.linkedin.com/in/viktorfarcic/) ([DevOps Toolkit](https://www.youtube.com/@DevOpsToolkit)) and [Whitney Lee](https://www.linkedin.com/in/whitneylee/) ([whitneylee.com](https://whitneylee.com/)) in person. Been attending every European KubeCon since.

## The Homelab

Ever since I discovered Linux, I've always had a Linux box (once FreeBSD) as a homelab and test playground. The infra grew over time as my hunger to test out new things grew.

What started as "I should learn Kubernetes" turned into a 3-node Proxmox cluster running 90+ applications. It's my playground for testing ideas — many of which become real implementations at work.

## Passions

**Linux** — Started in the early 2000s. Spent countless nights reading, testing, debugging. My first install had no GPU drivers, no sound — didn't care. I was hooked.

**Kubernetes** — Felt the same way when I discovered it. Both Linux and Kubernetes are foundations for building platforms and solutions. Same energy, different era.

**Agentic AI** — The latest obsession. Always felt like I needed to hire someone to help manage the ever-growing homelab. I mostly skipped chat-based AI; it hallucinated too much, and I was too lazy to copy-paste back and forth between the terminal and a chat window. Then in August 2025 I discovered Claude Code and upgraded to the $200/month Max plan. That's when AI really became a thing for me. Agentic AI that runs in your terminal, reads your code, executes commands, and tests its own work is a completely different experience. Done huge migrations and implementations that would've taken forever, or projects I wouldn't have even started. Now I have a teammate that is never tired.

**Honorable mention: Internal Developer Platforms** — Always believed DevOps engineers (I know it is a philosophy and not a job title) shouldn't become the bottleneck that sysadmins once were for developers. When IDPs became a thing ([BACK Stack](https://backstack.dev/intro/)), it clicked. I think of them as custom-tailored cloud consoles — just like AWS/GCP abstracts infrastructure, IDPs offer golden paths and self-service for devs without drowning in operational complexity or waiting on Ops team.
